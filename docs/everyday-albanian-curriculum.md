# Everyday Albanian inside the adventure

The game’s practical-language promise is simple: a beginner should gain useful social power quickly, but every phrase must belong honestly to the world and its story. The curriculum does not become a detached travel phrasebook.

## What “quickly” means

The opening conversation now teaches a transferable interaction skeleton in its first five choices:

1. greet someone and answer “How are you?”;
2. say where you are going;
3. accept or decline a plan;
4. ask someone to wait, admit “I don’t know,” and choose a meeting place;
5. agree on today or tomorrow and a time.

The first village errand continues the same conversation instead of resetting into a lesson. The player agrees to help prepare for a guest, follows an instruction, asks where the market and guest-room are, follows left/right directions, asks a fast speaker to slow down or repeat, buys bread and salt, and welcomes a traveller. The supper then becomes a genuine social scene: permission to enter, offering food and water, a small favour, introductions, home and work, family, missing home, the journey, weather, tiredness, sympathy, news, follow-up questions, disbelief, fear, opinions, disagreement and a consequential decision. That decision opens the Kulshedra trail. The square’s birthday gathering teaches wishes, natural reactions and an explicit joke marker. The café carries work, sleep and next-day talk. Later, the established Gjakova inn and healer carry room, price, coffee, pain, help, and doctor language without importing a modern airport, bus, or phone into the folktale world.

This gives the player the main intent and routine phrases in the private birthday-chat sample during the first village visit. It does not promise instant comprehension of names, code-switching, one-off misspellings, or a vulgar wordplay punchline. Those are genuine parts of chat literacy, but they should not displace reusable Albanian.

## Curriculum contract

The public curriculum currently contains:

- 19 opening targets;
- 60 first-session targets;
- 144 practice-priority senses;
- 97 whole-phrase drills;
- 20 story-grounded can-do groups.

Every whole-phrase drill must be found in a playable story line or action. `scripts/conversationaudit.mjs` rejects an ungrounded drill, a missing first-session target, an unknown sense, or an underfilled can-do group.

Practice prioritizes complete useful chunks and the common words inside them. It teaches the clear, uncompressed form a learner can speak and write. Texting abbreviations do not consume practice time; once the learner knows the underlying language, real conversations can supply those local habits naturally.

## Breadth audit

The audit is organized around what a person can do with language, not around arbitrary themed word lists.

| Conversational job | Where it belongs in the story |
|---|---|
| greet, thank, apologize and say goodbye | bridge, market, guest-room and square |
| introduce yourself; ask name, origin, home and work | guest supper and the foreign traveller |
| ask about family and say someone is missed | guest supper, kurbet and family legends |
| locate people and make plans | opening bridge exchange, square and café |
| invite, accept, decline and ask permission | bridge plan and guest-room threshold |
| catch up, exchange news and ask what happened | guest supper, café and village spring |
| keep a conversation moving | `Vërtet?`, `Pastaj?`, `Më trego…`, `Nuk e di` and repair phrases |
| express hunger, thirst, tiredness, fear, pain and concern | supper, road danger and healer |
| ask opinions, agree and disagree | the choice between the dry well and the old woman |
| request a favour, respond and give a simple instruction | bread-and-salt errand and table |
| discuss weather | the traveller’s wet road, cold forest and rain ritual |
| talk about eating, sleeping and work | guest table, late café, market, mill and kurbet |
| ask for help or care | opening errand, healer and danger scenes |
| ask and follow directions | market errand, roads, gates and signs |
| buy, compare prices and handle simple services | market, inn, gate and healer |
| offer, order and discuss food preferences | guest table, market, inn and feasts |
| find lodging | Gjakova inn |
| understand celebration and playful tone | birthday gathering, wedding and Nastradin stories |

This also covers the recurring topic families found in natural-conversation research: self-report, other people’s news, plans, household tasks, work, food, family, personal experience, weather, leisure, humour, affection, conflict and storytelling. The surface objects change to remain in-world: a house party becomes a feast or wedding, a car journey becomes a road or horse journey, a music discussion becomes song, dance or lahuta, and an accommodation dispute becomes an inn or guest-room problem. The communicative function transfers even when the modern object does not.

No finite story can cover every possible daily topic, proper name, job, meme or private reference. The release claim is narrower and testable: it covers the portable beginner conversational functions that can honestly occur in this world, gives the highest-value ones early practice, and rejects a drill that has no playable story line.

## Lore boundary

A phrase enters only when a character has a believable reason to say it and saying it has a consequence.

- Roads, springs, markets, guest-rooms, inns, coffee, healers, work, celebrations, danger, and hospitality belong naturally.
- A modern facility can enter only if the world itself grows to contain it coherently.
- Buses, airports, Wi-Fi, bank cards, phones, pharmacies, and ambulances remain out while they have no honest place in this setting.
- Contemporary chat informs which ordinary meanings deserve priority. It does not overwrite standard Albanian or turn private spelling habits or jokes into the syllabus.

The practical quest uses bread-and-salt hospitality as the hinge into lore: ordinary help earns entry to the guest-room; the guest’s road news points back toward the dry well and the Kulshedra. Practical language is therefore how the player reaches the myth, not a pause from it.

## Research basis

The task order follows the CEFR progression from A1 immediate needs, familiar questions and reactions into A2 introductions, news, feelings, favours, invitations, suggestions, apologies, preferences, opinions, travel, directions, shopping, meals and simple problems. The A2 telecommunications descriptors independently emphasize exchanging simple news, making plans and arranging to meet—the same functions that dominate the private chat sample. See the [Council of Europe CEFR descriptor search](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search) and [CEFR Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2018/1680787989.pdf).

The domain mix also agrees with the [Peace Corps Albanian language lessons](https://files.peacecorps.gov/multimedia/audio/languagelessons/albania/AL_Albanian_Language_Lessons.pdf): greetings, name/origin/home/work questions, feelings, help, opinions, food preferences, prices, directions, family, numbers, hospitality and social rituals recur because they carry ordinary life.

Topic coverage is checked against natural conversation rather than imagined phrasebook situations. Daily-conversation studies repeatedly surface self-report, other-report, household-task talk, humour, plans, narratives, positivity and conflict; relationship diaries add catching up, affection, family and private concerns. See [Mapping the topography of couples’ daily conversation](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0265407505050941) and [What Do Romanian Couples Talk About on a Daily Basis?](https://www.rcis.ro/en/section1/155-volumul-672019decembrie/2644-what-do-romanian-couples-talk-about-on-a-daily-basis-a-mixed-method-approach.html). These are used as breadth checks, not as Albanian frequency evidence.

Whole phrases receive heavy practice because formulaic sequences are processed faster and with fewer errors than matched non-formulaic language in Jiang and Nekrasova’s study, [“The Processing of Formulaic Sequences by Second Language Speakers”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-4781.2007.00589.x). Tasks are embedded in story consequences because beginner task-based work can be built around meaning-focused input; see Rod Ellis, [“Using tasks with beginner-level learners”](https://benjamins.com/catalog/ltyl.19005.ell).

The game teaches clear standard forms while accepting that real Albanian varies by speaker and region. Mozilla’s [Spontaneous Speech Dataset for Gheg Albanian](https://mozilladatacollective.com/datasets/cmqi2ikzm006cmf07yam3i21d) is useful research evidence for that variation, but dialect compression is not a required practice track in the game.

## Measurement

The private corpus remains ignored by version control and is never a build dependency. Its current aggregate baseline is 727 messages and 3,789 word tokens. The game directly covers 58.7% of token occurrences and 63.8% after conservative chat normalization; among the 100 most frequent corpus word types, it covers 90.4% of their occurrences.

Those figures measure lexical overlap, not full understanding. Progress should be judged on two levels:

1. **intent:** can the player tell what people are doing—greeting, inviting, teasing, arranging, thanking?;
2. **reusable lines:** can the player understand and produce the complete high-value phrase?;

Names, English code-switches, abbreviations, typos, rare slang and joke-specific vocabulary stay visible in research totals but do not automatically become game content. Decoding those is a later real-world skill built on the standard language the game teaches.
